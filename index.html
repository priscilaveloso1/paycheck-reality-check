    Your real number

    Most of us plan around the paycheck we wish we had. Let's find the real one. Everything that lands in your account counts as income. But only income that repeats should be used to plan. A bonus, a garage sale or a gift is welcome, but don't build your life around it.

      Cheaper ways out

      Check the current terms. Rules and rates change, and in Canada each province sets its own.

      • United States: credit union Payday Alternative Loans (PALs). Federal credit unions can offer them under NCUA rules: up to $2,000, repaid over 1 to 12 months, with the rate capped at 28% APR and an application fee of at most $20. You have to be a member, so ask a credit union you can join. Read the rule.
      • Canada: the cost of a payday loan. In most provinces the maximum is $14 for every $100 borrowed, which FCAC says works out to roughly 365% a year. Cheaper options it lists: more time to pay a bill, a pay advance from your employer, a line of credit or overdraft protection at your bank, or a payment deferral from your lender.
      • Stuck in debt? Talk to a nonprofit credit counsellor. In Canada, FCAC points to Credit Counselling Canada (creditcounsellingcanada.ca) and the Canadian Association for Financial Empowerment (cafe-acaf.org). It also says no reputable agency charges for the first meeting, and that talking to one doesn't affect your credit score. In the US, look for a nonprofit agency, for example one that belongs to the National Foundation for Credit Counseling (nfcc.org). Read the FCAC guide.
